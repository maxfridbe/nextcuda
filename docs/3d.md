# 3D

`model3d.generate` (a mesh from a picture, or - given a `mesh` or a `file` made here - that mesh painted like the
picture) and `model3d.rig`. The models are in [models.md](models.md): TRELLIS.2, Hunyuan3D-2mini and 2.1, UniRig,
SkinTokens, RigNet, AnimateAnyMesh, UniMate; settings in [environment.md](environment.md) (`NS_M3D_*`).

## Hunyuan3D 2.1 paint on its own

`hunyuan3d-2.1-paint` is 2.1's paint model without its shape model: the 2.5D multiview UNet draws six views of albedo and
metallic-roughness (15 UniPC steps) that are baked into a UV atlas. Give `model3d.generate` a mesh (from any engine) and
the picture it should look like - `nextcuda model3d gen PICTURE --mesh FILE.glb` - and it comes back painted. The full
`hunyuan3d-2.1` paints the shape it makes the same way.

- **On a 12 GB card** (2026-10-21): the weights are 5.9 GiB, the scratch arenas are sized from the card's free memory
  (`NS_M3D_HY_PAINT_KEEP_MIB`, `_TMP_MIB`; at least the measured peaks) and DINOv2-giant and the reference UNet are
  loaded for a run's conditioning and let go before its 15-step loop - the one model here that moves its weights
  (`NS_M3D_HY_PAINT_TRACE=1` prints the card's free memory at each phase). A 40k-face fox: the 5070 83.9 s at 11.4 of
  12.2 GiB, the 3060 190.6 s at 11.8 of 12.3 GiB - nothing else may be on the card. Its card's `vram_gib: 10.9` says
  so: the front door counts it as the whole card (capped at the card less its margin) and stops the card's idle models
  before it loads (an entry pulled before 2026-10-22 carries 0: set it in `models.json` or pull again).
- It is a different model from the Hunyuan3D-2 paint (mmgp) the platform used before: PBR output, no GGUF either way.

## SkinTokens and UniMate: rig a mesh, then walk it

```sh
nextcuda model3d rig mesh.glb --model skintokens --out rigged.glb                 # a skeleton and skin weights
nextcuda model3d rig rigged.glb --model unimate --prompt "An object walks forward." --out walk.glb   # the skeleton in motion
```

Both are nextsycl's ports (model3d/skintokens, model3d/unimate; nextsycl's docs/3d.md has the models in full) with their
kernels in CUDA, float32 throughout, checked stage by stage on the 5070 against reference/skintokens/ref.py and
reference/unimate/ref.py (float32 CPU torch, the checkpoints read by a pickle reader that allows only tensors):
`nextcuda model3d check <dump dir> --model skintokens --mesh humanoid_lo.obj` /
`--model unimate --mesh unitree_go2.glb --stages t5,pass,ode,dec,prep`.

The silos (CONTRIBUTING rule 5). SkinTokens' (kernels/model3d/skintokens/silo): a one-pass float32 attention for heads
of 16 to 64 (a thread a query row, key and value tiles of 32 broadcast from shared memory, flash attention's online
softmax) for the encoders' 54,000 keys and the skin VAE's 54,000 queries; the decode step's attention over the beams'
slot pool (a block a beam and key / value head, a warp a key row); the decode step's products from the checkpoint's own
bfloat16 weights. UniMate's (kernels/model3d/unimate/silo): its attention a block a (group, head), the keys and values in
shared memory. The checks above are with the silos; NS_SILO=0 gives the same numbers within 1e-7.

| UniMate, the Go2 example, "An object walks forward." (110-146 evaluations) | 8.4-10.8 s (13.4 s without the silo) | 19.6 s | 1.0 GiB |

The time of a request is the engine's; loading takes 4-8 s more (SkinTokens 1.1 GB, UniMate 1.2 + 1.0 GB read from the
disk), longer when the files are not in the page cache. When the card has too little free memory for SkinTokens'
beams it says so ("not enough free memory ... for the decoder's cache") instead of failing in a kernel; `--opt-beams 1`
needs less. Both stage checks pass on the 3060 too (SkinTokens worst 5.0e-5 with the same tokens; UniMate worst 1.3e-6,
the flow 8.7e-7 with the same 128 evaluations).

UniMate's time is the flow (each evaluation a guided and an unguided pass over 71 joints x 61 tokens); the mesh does
not matter. What is left there is the float32 cuBLAS products (5 of 12 s under nsys).

**Looked at** (reference/unimate/render_anim.py poses the GLB by linear blend skinning and draws frames 0, 15, 30, 45
from the side and the front; verify_anim.py measures the clip), 2026-10-27, "An object walks forward.":
- the Go2 example as UniMate ships it (real joint names): a trot, every leg stepping, 3.6 units forward in 2 s;
- a textured soldier from the platform rigged by SkinTokens (22 joints, `bone_N`): seed 1 a clean walk - alternate
  strides, the arms swinging - but nearly on the spot (0.15 units); seed 2 a longer stride that travels a unit, partly
  backwards; the joints turn 30-50 degrees;
- the Go2's mesh rigged by SkinTokens (20 joints): the facing guessed from the mirror symmetry was backwards (the walk
  went -Z); `--opt-facing +z` sends it forward 3.8 (seed 1) and 1.8 units (seed 2), but its legs hardly move - the
  body glides. With generic names the motion is weak and unstable, as model-cards/guides/unimate.md says.

The mesh crate now reads a skinned mesh without its own node's transform (glTF 2.0: its vertices are in the bind space
of the inverse bind matrices). Before, the Go2 example's parts, under a Z-up node, came out turned 90 degrees away from
their skeleton - which no number caught (the skeleton is read from the joints), only the picture. The 3D page's viewer
plays a skeletal animation (the mesh posed on the CPU each frame) as well as morph targets.
